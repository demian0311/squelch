# 1.25 m

222–225 MHz, a band only the Americas have. A few repeaters, loyal users, and mostly silence.

## What you will hear

- FM repeaters, often linked to a club's 2 m or 70 cm machine so the same conversation appears here.
- 223.5 MHz simplex, the calling frequency.
- SSB and Morse near 222.1 MHz on contest weekends.
- Radios for this band are rare, which is why it is quiet; some hams keep it for that.

## How this radio reads it

Narrow FM. Repeaters talk 1.6 MHz above their inputs. The whole band fits in two captures.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 222.100 | SSB and Morse calling |
| 222.250–223.380 | FM repeater inputs |
| 223.400–223.520 | FM simplex, calling at 223.500 |
| 223.850–224.980 | FM repeater outputs |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 97](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-97). Listening needs nothing.
- Transmitting needs an amateur licence. [Technician](https://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed), the first of three classes, is a 35-question exam, no Morse, $35 to the FCC for ten years, and it covers this whole band.
- A station sends its callsign at least every ten minutes and at the end of a contact (97.119), so you always learn who is talking.
- Up to 1.5 kW is legal, but a repeater runs tens of watts and a handheld five.
