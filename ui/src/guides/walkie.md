# Walkie-talkies

The 22 channels in every blister-pack radio, on 462 and 467 MHz. FRS needs nothing; GMRS is the same channels with more power, repeaters and a licence.

## What you will hear

- Families at a park, kids, hunters, a crew on a job site, a ski group: short and close by, a mile or two.
- GMRS repeaters on hilltops carry a club or a neighbourhood across a county. The repeater's output is on channels 15–22 and you hear everyone through it.
- Privacy codes (CTCSS tones) keep other people's chatter out of a radio; they hide nothing from this receiver.

## How this radio reads it

Narrow FM. The 462 MHz and 467 MHz halves are 5 MHz apart, more than the dongle hears at once, so the Channels panel shows one side at a time and the other side fades. A repeater listens 5 MHz above where it talks: 467.600 in, 462.600 out.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 462.5625–462.7125 | Ch 1–7 · FRS 2 W, GMRS 5 W |
| 467.5625–467.7125 | Ch 8–14 · 0.5 W, hand-held only |
| 462.5500–462.7250 | Ch 15–22 · FRS 2 W, GMRS 50 W, repeater outputs |
| 467.5500–467.7250 | Ch 15R–22R · GMRS repeater inputs |
| 12.5 kHz | Channel width |

## Rules

- FCC Part 95: FRS is [subpart B](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-95/subpart-B), GMRS [subpart E](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-95/subpart-E). Listening needs nothing.
- FRS: no licence, 2 W (0.5 W on 8–14), the antenna fixed to the radio.
- GMRS: a licence, $35 for ten years, no exam, anyone 18 or over, and it covers the whole family. Up to 50 W on the main channels and repeaters.
- Anyone may use any channel in an emergency.
