# Sensors 433

Weather stations, wireless thermometers, doorbells, tyre sensors on imported cars and a thousand other gadgets, on 433.92 MHz. Short bursts, no licence.

## What you will hear

- Nothing by ear. On the waterfall a weather station is a dot every 30–60 s, a thermometer the same, a doorbell a dot when pressed. A neighbourhood is a few columns of dots.
- Garage openers, remote switches, energy monitors, and the fobs of cars built for Europe or Asia.
- Any ham voice here is the 70 cm band, which this slice sits inside.

![A sensor is a column of dots on the waterfall](diagram/ism-burst)

## How this radio reads it

On-off keying or simple FSK, each maker's own layout. This receiver shows the bursts on the waterfall and does not yet decode them; the rtl_433 project reads several hundred device types, and a weather station's temperature is a typical first decode.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 433.920 | The centre most devices use |
| 433.05–434.79 | The ISM band these devices share (inside the 70 cm ham band) |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 15.231](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/15.231): unlicensed control signals, short bursts only, no voice. The device must accept interference and cause none.
- Listening needs nothing. The band is also the amateur 70 cm band, where hams come first.
