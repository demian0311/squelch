# 33 cm

902–928 MHz, shared with every cordless phone, smart meter and LoRa gadget in the country. Hams have it second: a few repeaters and the weak-signal crowd.

## What you will hear

- FM repeaters near 927 MHz, with inputs 25 MHz below at 902 MHz. Few, mostly in big cities.
- SSB and Morse at 903.1 MHz on contest weekends.
- Mostly the ISM devices: meters, hopping data links, baby monitors: [915 MHz devices](band:ism915)

## How this radio reads it

Narrow FM. The band is 26 MHz, more than ten captures, so the waterfall shows a slice. Ham signals are narrow lines that come and go; the ISM noise is wide and busy.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 902.000–903.000 | Repeater inputs |
| 902.100 · 903.100 | Weak-signal calling (SSB, Morse) |
| 909–927 | Broadband: data, digital video |
| 927.000–928.000 | Repeater outputs, inputs 25 MHz below |
| 927.500 | FM simplex calling |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 97](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-97). Listening needs nothing.
- Transmitting needs an amateur licence. [Technician](https://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed), the first of three classes, is a 35-question exam, no Morse, $35 to the FCC for ten years, and it covers this whole band.
- A station sends its callsign at least every ten minutes and at the end of a contact (97.119), so you always learn who is talking.
- Up to 1.5 kW is legal, but a repeater runs tens of watts and a handheld five.
- Amateur use is secondary: hams must accept interference from the ISM devices and from government radar, and may not cause it.
