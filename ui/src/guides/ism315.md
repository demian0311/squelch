# Sensors 315

Car key fobs, tyre-pressure sensors, garage door openers and alarm sensors, on 315 MHz in North America. Short bursts, no licence, no permission.

## What you will hear

- Nothing by ear: a burst lasts a fraction of a second. On the waterfall a fob is a few dots when someone locks a car in the car park; a tyre sensor is a dot every minute or so from each wheel while the car moves.
- Door and window sensors, driveway alarms, remote switches: a dot whenever something happens.
- The rest of the world puts these devices on 433.92 MHz; an imported car or gadget shows up there instead.

![A sensor is a column of dots on the waterfall](diagram/ism-burst)

## How this radio reads it

On-off keying or simple FSK, a few hundred bits a burst, each maker's own layout. This receiver shows the bursts on the waterfall and does not yet decode them; the rtl_433 project reads several hundred device types.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 315.0 | North American key fobs, tyre sensors, remotes |
| 310–318 | Where the cheap crystals in them actually land |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 15.231](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/15.231): unlicensed control signals, short bursts only, no voice, no continuous transmission. Every such device must accept interference and cause none.
- Listening needs nothing. Replaying a fob's signal to open a car does not go through this receiver, and is a crime.
