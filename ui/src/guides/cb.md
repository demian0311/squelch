# CB radio

40 public AM channels around 27 MHz. No licence, since 1983. Truckers, farms, off-roaders and hobbyists.

## What you will hear

- Channel 19 is the highway: truckers on road conditions, speed traps, and where the traffic stops.
- Channel 9 is kept for emergencies and road help by rule; often silent.
- Most other channels are quiet locally. When the sun is active (2024–2026 is a peak of its 11-year cycle) "skip" bounces 27 MHz off the sky and you hear stations a thousand miles away, all at once.
- Channels 36–40 are single sideband by custom: tune them with LSB or the voice sounds like a duck.

## How this radio reads it

AM, channels 10 kHz apart. Two stations at once both come through, garbled, which is how CB sounds in a crowd. The whole band fits in one capture, so the Channels panel shows all 40 tiles and lights the ones on air. FM has been allowed on CB since 2021; an FM voice on an AM channel sounds hollow, so switch the mode if it does.

## Rules

- [FCC Part 95 subpart D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-95/subpart-D). No licence to listen or to talk.
- 4 W carrier on AM and FM, 12 W peak on sideband. No amplifiers. Antenna no higher than 60 ft above ground.
- Channel 9 is only for emergencies and traveller assistance. Keep a conversation under five minutes, then a minute off.
- The old rule against talking to stations over 155 miles away was dropped in 2017: skip is legal now.
