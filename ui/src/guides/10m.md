# 10 m

The amateur band at 28–29.7 MHz, the top of shortwave. Dead for years at a time, then, when the sun is active, the whole world on a wire antenna.

## What you will hear

- Single sideband voice between 28.3 and 28.5 MHz, where Technician licensees have their only shortwave voice privilege: contests, "CQ DX", a callsign from another continent.
- Morse and digital modes (FT8 at 28.074) at the bottom of the band, beacons around 28.2–28.3 that say how far the band is open.
- FM repeaters and simplex near 29.6 MHz.
- All of it depends on the sun: in the 11-year cycle's peak years (2024–2026) the band opens most days; near the minimum it is empty.

## How this radio reads it

Upper sideband is the mode for voice below 29 MHz; the band enters in USB. Tune in 100 Hz steps until the voice sounds right. Morse and digital show as thin lines; the receiver does not decode them. The tuner's lower edge is 24 MHz; the bands below it ([20 m](band:20m) and down) go through the dongle's direct sampling path instead.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 28.000–28.070 | Morse |
| 28.070–28.150 | Digital (FT8 at 28.074) |
| 28.200–28.300 | Beacons |
| 28.300–28.500 | Voice, SSB; Technician privileges end at 28.5 |
| 28.500–29.300 | Voice, SSB and AM (General and above) |
| 29.600 | FM simplex calling |
| 29.610–29.700 | FM repeater outputs, inputs 100 kHz below |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 97](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-97). Listening needs nothing.
- Transmitting needs an amateur licence. [Technician](https://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed), the first of three classes, is a 35-question exam, no Morse, $35 to the FCC for ten years, and it covers this whole band.
- A station sends its callsign at least every ten minutes and at the end of a contact (97.119), so you always learn who is talking.
- Up to 1.5 kW is legal, but a repeater runs tens of watts and a handheld five.
- On 10 m a Technician may use 28.0–28.5 MHz at up to 200 W.
