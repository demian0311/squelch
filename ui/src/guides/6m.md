# 6 m

50–54 MHz, "the magic band": quiet most days, then the air itself opens and a signal from a thousand miles away appears for an hour.

## What you will hear

- FM repeaters and simplex above 51 MHz: local, like 2 m but less busy.
- SSB and Morse near 50.1–50.3 MHz, with 50.125 the calling frequency, when the band opens: sporadic-E in early summer, meteor scatter at dawn.
- FT8 at 50.313 is where most of the activity is now; it shows as a stack of thin lines.
- Beacons at 50.06–50.08 say where the band is open from.

## How this radio reads it

Narrow FM for the repeaters, USB for the low end. Repeaters are 500 kHz or 1 MHz apart from their inputs. The whole band is 4 MHz, nearly two captures.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 50.000–50.100 | Morse and beacons |
| 50.100–50.300 | SSB, calling at 50.125 |
| 50.313 | FT8 |
| 51.620–51.980 | FM repeater outputs |
| 52.525 | FM simplex calling |
| 52.5–53.98 | FM repeater outputs, inputs 500 kHz below |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 97](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-97). Listening needs nothing.
- Transmitting needs an amateur licence. [Technician](https://www.arrl.org/getting-licensed), the first of three classes, is a 35-question exam, no Morse, $35 to the FCC for ten years, and it covers this whole band.
- A station sends its callsign at least every ten minutes and at the end of a contact (97.119), so you always learn who is talking.
- Up to 1.5 kW is legal, but a repeater runs tens of watts and a handheld five.
