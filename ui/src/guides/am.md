# AM radio

Broadcast stations on 535–1705 kHz: news, talk, sports and religion, each on a 10 kHz channel. The oldest broadcast band, and at night the farthest-reaching one on this receiver.

## What you will hear

- A station every 10 kHz from 540 to 1700 kHz. By day the ones within about 100 miles; after dark the band fills with stations from half the continent, fading in and out as the sky bounces them.
- The big stations run 50 kW, and the band is where the oldest call signs live.
- Static from storms, buzz from power lines and chargers, and a hum under everything: the band carries noise the way FM never does.

## How this radio reads it

The dongle's tuner stops at 24 MHz. Below it the dongle samples the antenna straight (direct sampling), and the tuner's gain and its filters are out of the path: the antenna alone sets the level, so Settings shows no signal boost here. A whip meant for FM and aircraft is a poor AM antenna; a long wire, even one laid along a wall, hears far more. The capture reaches down to 0 Hz, so the whole band fits in one view.

## Band plan

| kHz | What |
|---|---|
| 540–1600 | The original channels |
| 1610–1700 | The expanded band, added in the 1990s |
| 10 kHz | Channel spacing |
| 530, 1610, 1620… | Traveller's information stations (10 W), near airports and parks |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 73](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/part-73): the band is [535–1705 kHz](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/73.14), carriers 10 kHz apart from 540 to 1700. Listening needs nothing.
- Many stations drop power or sign off at sunset so distant stations can be heard; the ones that stay at full power are the clear-channel stations.

## Try first

[Tune 850](tune:0.85/am) kHz after dark and step through the band in 10 kHz steps; a station from a thousand miles away is ordinary on a winter night.
