# 915 MHz devices

902–928 MHz, the unlicensed band for anything that hops or spreads: LoRa and Meshtastic nodes, smart electricity and water meters, baby monitors, old cordless phones. A busy, wide, noisy place.

## What you will hear

- Nothing by ear. On the waterfall: meters as short bursts across the band (the utility's truck reads them as it drives by), hopping links as confetti, LoRa as a wide chirp that sweeps up or down.
- A Meshtastic mesh: a chirp every few seconds from each node in range, 250 kHz wide.
- Hams share this band: a narrow FM line near 927 MHz is one. [33 cm](band:33cm)

## How this radio reads it

Spread spectrum: a signal that hops between channels or sweeps across one. The waterfall shows the shapes; this receiver decodes none of them. The band is 26 MHz, more than ten captures, so drag the waterfall to look around.

## Band plan

| MHz | What |
|---|---|
| 902–928 | ISM band, Part 15 spread spectrum and hopping |
| 903–927 | LoRa and Meshtastic, in 125–500 kHz channels |
| 915 | Smart meters and many remotes sit near the middle |

## Rules

- [FCC Part 15.247](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/15.247): unlicensed spread-spectrum devices up to 1 W. Each must accept interference and cause none.
- Listening needs nothing. The band is shared with hams (secondary) and government radar (primary).
